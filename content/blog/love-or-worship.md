There's a difference between loving something and worshipping it.

Love holds its object clearly — aware of its flaws, present to its beauty, committed to its growth. Worship, on the other hand, places its object beyond question. Criticism becomes heresy. Doubt becomes betrayal.

This distinction matters more than most people realize, especially when it comes to how we build things.

## When Admiration Becomes Distortion

I've watched founders worship their own ideas. Not love them — worship them. And the difference is catastrophic.

When you love your idea, you hold it with open hands. You let users tell you it's wrong. You pivot when the evidence demands it. You kill features that aren't working.

When you worship your idea, you explain away the data. You fire the people who push back. You double down on the thing that isn't working because admitting it doesn't work would require you to admit something uncomfortable about yourself.

The startup landscape is littered with the wreckage of worshipped ideas.

## The Tech Industry's False Gods

There's a version of this that's particularly acute in technology.

We've built an entire culture around worshipping founders — treating their every utterance as prophecy, their every failure as a learning moment worthy of a TED talk, their every success as evidence of genius rather than timing, luck, and compounding advantage.

This isn't love. It's a kind of secular religion. And like all religions built on false gods, it eventually produces disillusionment — or worse, complicity.

The antidote isn't cynicism. It's discernment.

## Building From Love

The best builders I know love their work without worshipping it.

They love the problem they're solving. They love the people they're solving it for. They love the craft of building — the code, the design, the systems.

But they don't worship any of it. Which means they can change it when they need to.

They can look at a product they've spent years building and say: "This doesn't work for the people it's supposed to serve. What does?"

That's freedom. And it comes from love, not worship.

## The Question Worth Asking

Whatever you're building, whoever you're following, whatever community you're part of — ask yourself: am I loving this, or am I worshipping it?

The answer will tell you a lot about how you'll respond when it asks something hard of you.

Love endures. Worship collapses.

Build from love.
