Eagles don't flock.

You'll never see a hundred eagles circling together above a field. That's pigeons. Eagles operate alone, at altitude, with precision.

I've been thinking about this a lot as I watch how different people approach building in Africa.

## The Altitude Problem

Most people are trained for ground-level operations. School, employment, social structures — they all optimize for coordination at scale. Follow the rules. Stay in formation. Don't rise too far above the group.

This is useful. Civilisation depends on it.

But it creates a problem for anyone who wants to build something new. The skills that make you a good employee — compliance, consistency, deference to hierarchy — are often the exact opposite of what's needed to start and grow something from zero.

Eagles don't wait for permission to hunt.

## What Operating at Altitude Actually Means

It doesn't mean arrogance. It doesn't mean going it alone or refusing help.

It means developing the perspective that comes from height. When you're at altitude, you can:

- See patterns that aren't visible from the ground
- Identify leverage points before others do
- Hold a long view while still executing in the present

Most people never develop this because it requires them to temporarily leave the comfort of the flock — and that's terrifying.

## The Uganda Context

Building in Uganda comes with specific challenges that make altitude-thinking even harder to develop.

Risk is expensive here. Capital is scarce. The safety nets that exist in more developed markets — venture debt, angel networks, unemployment insurance — don't exist in the same way.

So people stay close to the ground. Take the government job. Build the service business. Optimize for survival over asymmetric upside.

I understand this. I've made those calculations myself.

But I also believe that Africa's next wave of builders will be eagles — people who've learned to see from height, who've internalized the risk, and who've found ways to operate at altitude without losing touch with the ground reality of the people they're building for.

## The Question

Could you be an eagle?

Not: are you better than others? Not: do you deserve more?

Just: are you willing to develop the perspective, take on the solitude, and operate at the altitude that the work requires?

The sky has plenty of room. But first you have to leave the ground.
